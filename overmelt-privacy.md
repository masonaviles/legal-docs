# Privacy Policy — Overmelt

_Last updated: 5 October 2026_

Overmelt is an app for iPhone and Android that compares the price of gold, silver and platinum
jewelry with the value of the metal in it. It is published by Marian Aviles. This policy explains
what the app keeps on your phone, the few things it sends, and who receives them.

## Summary

**We never receive your data.** Your collection, photos, notes, prices and appraisals are stored
on your phone and are not sent to us or to anyone else. There is no account and no sign-up, and
there are no ads.

The app does send four kinds of request, described below: it asks a price service for the day's
metal prices, it sends crash reports that you can turn off, it sends usage statistics only if you
turn them on, and it checks your purchase of Overmelt Pro with the app store.

## What stays on your phone

Everything you enter or attach is stored only in the app's own storage on your device:

- pieces you appraise, save or compare, with their weights, prices, sellers, dates and notes
- photos and video clips you take or choose, and links you add
- groups, tags, alerts and settings
- the history of metal prices the app has seen, used to draw your collection's value over time

None of this is uploaded by the app. If your phone backs up to iCloud or Google, your backup may
include the app's data; that backup belongs to your iCloud or Google account, and we cannot read
it. Backups and exports you make from inside the app go wherever you choose to save or send them.

Notifications and price alerts are scheduled on your phone. Home screen widgets read their figures
from the app on your phone.

## What the app sends, and to whom

### Metal prices

To show today's gold, silver and platinum prices, the app requests them from
[gold-api.com](https://gold-api.com). The request names only the metal. As with any internet
request, the service can see your device's IP address. You can enter your own price for a metal
instead, and the app then stops requesting that metal's price.

### Crash reports — on, and you can turn them off

When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a report to
[Sentry](https://sentry.io), a crash-reporting service, so the fault can be found and fixed. A
report contains:

- the type of error and where in the app's code it happened
- the screen it happened on, by its name in the app (never what was on it)
- your phone's model, its operating system version and its language setting
- the app's version
- a random identifier that Sentry creates for this installation of the app, and records of when
  the app was opened, which are used to count how many sessions end in a crash

A report never contains prices, weights, sellers, names, notes, photos or anything you typed. The
app removes those before a report is sent, and it does not send the trail of taps and screens that
crash reporters usually collect.

To stop crash reports, turn off **Send crash reports** in Settings → Privacy. The app then stops
the reporting service entirely, including for crashes inside the app's native code.

### Usage statistics — off unless you turn them on

After your third appraisal, the app asks once whether you will share anonymous usage statistics.
They are off unless you say yes, and you can change your answer in Settings → Privacy at any time.

If you turn them on, the app sends short event records to [PostHog](https://posthog.com), an
analytics service, so we can see how the app is used and check whether its grades match real
prices. The events are: an appraisal completed (the metal, the kind of piece, the grade it landed
in, and whether a length was entered), a warning shown (which one), a piece saved, Compare used
(how many pieces), a guide opened (which guide), the Pro screen shown, Pro bought, a payout
estimate made (the kind of buyer, how many pieces, and the offer as a range of the metal value),
an alert switched on (which kind), a document exported (which kind) and something shared into the
app (whether it was a link, text or an image).

An event never contains a price, a weight, a seller, a name, a photo or anything you typed. Each
time the app opens it creates a new random identifier for that session, so sessions cannot be
joined together, and PostHog is told not to build a profile of you or to work out your location
from your IP address.

### Overmelt Pro purchases

Overmelt Pro is bought through the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google handle the payment;
we never receive your card or payment details. To confirm that your purchase unlocks Pro, and to
restore it on a new phone, the app uses [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com), which receives
the store's purchase record and an anonymous identifier for this installation of the app. It does
not receive your name or email address.

## What we do not do

- We do not sell or rent data, and we do not share it for advertising.
- We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
- We do not use your data to make automated decisions about you.
- We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13; the app is not directed at
  them.

## How long data is kept

Data on your phone stays until you delete it or delete the app. Deleting the app deletes it from
the phone; backups you made are kept by whoever holds them.

Crash reports are kept by Sentry for up to 90 days. Usage statistics are kept by PostHog for up to
one year. Neither can be connected to you by name, because neither contains your name, email
address or any account.

## Your choices and rights

- Turn crash reports and usage statistics on or off in Settings → Privacy.
- Enter your own metal prices in Settings → Spot prices to stop requesting them.
- Delete everything by deleting the app.

Depending on where you live, you may have a right to ask what data about you is held, to have it
corrected or deleted, or to object to its use. Because we do not hold your name or any account,
the records described above cannot usually be found by asking for them by name, but you can
write to us at the address below and we will help as far as we can.
